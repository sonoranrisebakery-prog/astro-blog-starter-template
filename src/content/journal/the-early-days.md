It was a time of healthy-hippie whole-food blogs and green revolutions. My son Dereck would have been 3, almost 4, and my daughter Natasha had just been born. I was a newish dad, and at the same time I had started discovering the manufacturing processes behind commercial white flour.

It disgusted me and started me on a journey of, “My kids deserve better than that.”

I hopped on the healthy-hippie blog train and started real2live.com in August 2013. Around that same time, a friend introduced me to the idea of milling my own flour at home.

This is when I became “anti-white flour” and dove headfirst into the whole-grain revolution. There was even a movie someone was making about it that was supposed to come out, but I guess it never did.

The bleaching. The enriching. The stripping away of all the good, healthy stuff so flour could last longer on a store shelf.

I was one of those guys for a while. Washable diapers, anti-plastic—you know the stereotype. Haha.

These were the years when I started figuring out how to make sandwich loaves my kids would hopefully like that had zero white flour in them. That became an obsession.

Late rises. Failed, collapsing loaves. The classic whole-wheat loaf dense as a hockey puck. You name it.

After some tinkering, I settled on a hard red wheat that I could get in organic, non-GMO— all the labels — 25-pound bags from Amazon. I learned about presoaks, acid soaks, and ways of unlocking the wheat and letting it offer up more of its nutrition. Ferments. Slooow ferments that developed sour flavor while softening the wheat.

I bought some books and dove into *The Bread Baker’s Apprentice* and *Bread Science*, and just kept ingesting whatever I could about bread and whole grains.

The picture here is of some loaves from 2018, once I finally felt decently proud of the bread I was able to make.

As life got busier and responsibilities increased, the time I devoted to the blog dwindled and eventually disappeared. But I kept the whole bread thing alive.

By then, it had grown into something I was passionate about regardless.

It’s amazing the side quests that having children can send you down in life.

This is one of mine.
